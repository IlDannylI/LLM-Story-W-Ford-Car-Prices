How fast does a used Ford lose its value?

17,715 used Fords from one listing snapshot give us a cross-section of the market: cars of every age, mileage and engine, each with an asking price. We use them to ask what actually drives the price, how much comes from age versus mileage versus the car itself, and where a simple model stops being trustworthy.

What it shows

Median asking price of cars at each age. A model's line stops where fewer than 15 cars exist at an age, because a median of a handful of cars is noise.

How it is built

Cars are grouped by model and age (2020 minus model year). The median of each group is drawn with D3 as an SVG line. The cursor reveals the lines progressively.

Why this form

A line over age is the natural shape for change over time. Medians resist the few very expensive cars. The "% of year-1 price" toggle puts cheap and dear models on one comparable scale.

Middle: what is behind the curve?

Age and mileage rise together (they correlate at 0.72 in this data), so the curve in View 1 mixes the two. Views 2 and 3 pull them apart; Views 4 and 5 let you change one thing at a time.

What it shows

One dot per listing. Drag to rotate, scroll to zoom, hover to read a car's values, click a car to load it into Views 4 and 5.

How it is built

A hand-written 3D projection on an HTML canvas: rotation matrices, light perspective and a point-picking search. The surface is the age + mileage model from the prep step, drawn as a wireframe.

Why 3D

Three variables at once. The "Age×mileage" preset shows how tightly they travel together; the surface shows that the two alone leave a wide cloud of unexplained price.

Comparing price distributions between scenarios

Are two groups of cars really priced differently, or just differently spread?

What it shows

The share of cars at each price, one curve per group, for the cars that pass the filter bar. Hover to count cars near a price.

How it is built

A Gaussian kernel density with a fixed £800 bandwidth, computed in the browser for each group, plus the median and quartiles of each group.

Why this form

Overlapping densities show spread and overlap that a pair of averages hides. A fixed bandwidth keeps groups comparable.

View 4

Build a car and see what moves its price

Change one thing at a time. Which features add value and which take it away, compared with a typical Fiesta?

 

What it shows

The model's price estimate for the car you build, and a bar per feature: by what percentage that feature moves the price relative to the typical Fiesta.

How it is built

A regression of ln(price) on age, mileage, engine, mpg, tax, gearbox, fuel and model. Each bar is exp(coefficient × change): a multiplier, so bars do not depend on order.

Honest limits

The band is where 80% of training errors fell. "Similar cars" counts real listings near your settings; if there are none, the estimate is extrapolation and is flagged.

What it shows

The same car, re-priced as one variable sweeps across its range. The dot is the car you built in View 4 (or clicked in View 2 or 6).

How it is built

The model is evaluated 60 times along each axis with the other variables held at the car's values. Grey dots are a deterministic sample of real cars of that model and fuel.

Read with care

Real cars vary in engine and economy too, so the dots are a looser cloud than the line. Where there are no dots, the line is extrapolating.

Ending: how much can we trust it?

A model is evidence only if it is judged on cars it has not seen. 20% of the cars were held back, and every number below uses them.

What it shows

The same cars, scored by seven nested models. Step 0 guesses one price for every car; step 6 knows the age, mileage, engine, economy, tax, gearbox, fuel and model.

How it is built

Seven least-squares fits of ln(price) on the training 80% (Python, prep/prepare_data.py). The browser applies the saved coefficients to every row and scores any subset you filter.

Why this form

Predicted-vs-actual on held-out cars is the honest test. Bars give the size of the error; the ranked bars show which models are hardest to price.
